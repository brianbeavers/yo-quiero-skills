Team Onboarding

Goal is the same in every column: help new ops hires learn their first three workflows without a senior sitting with them all week. What changes is how far we push it.

MILD Safe and simple HOT A clear step up SPICY Bigger build, bigger bet INFERNO Swing for the fences
Mild

Checklist Home

Simple checklist. Easy to ship. Familiar to everyone.

One page with the three core workflows, links to the docs, and a mark-complete button. Managers see a basic progress percentage.

What you get

  • One screen: today's tasks plus linked SOPs
  • New hire marks items done (or reopens them)
  • Manager view: percent complete this week
  • Reuse components we already have

Build notes

  • No custom animation
  • Uses existing empty, loading, and error states

Tradeoff: Ships fast. Doesn't prove they actually learned the work.

About 2 to 4 days. Low risk.

Hot

Guided Tracks

More structure, still a normal product shape.

Three role tracks. Each module is read, practice, then a short quiz. Progress updates on its own where it can. Managers see status per track.

What you get

  • Pick a track, then work through modules
  • Short quiz before someone is marked certified
  • Manager board: who's stuck or overdue
  • Simple done state when a track finishes

Build notes

  • Light page transitions and a progress ring
  • One or two new list components

Tradeoff: Better sense of progress. Still mostly content and forms.

About 1 to 2 weeks. Content and quiz work.

Spicy

Live Practice

Practice in a fake version of the real tool, with coaching beside it.

Split screen: coach notes on the left, practice app on the right. The system checks their clicks. If they get stuck, they can call a real person.

What you get

  • Coach panel next to a practice copy of the app
  • Highlights tell them where to click
  • Call a coach when they're blocked
  • Manager timeline of attempts and trouble spots

Build notes

  • Highlights and click feedback
  • Needs a practice environment and event tracking

Tradeoff: Closer to real work. Harder to build and keep in sync.

About 1 to 2 sprints. Practice env is the hard part.

Inferno

Always-on Coach

Big swing. Help shows up in the live tool, not in a separate course.

A coach sits in the real app (with permission), steps in only when something looks risky, sends short drills overnight based on yesterday's misses, and tells managers when someone is ready to work alone. This is the stretch option.

What you get

  • Help inside the live tool, not a separate course site
  • Steps in at risky moments; easy to undo
  • Short drills overnight based on what went wrong
  • Manager view: ready or not, with a plain-language why

Build notes

  • Quiet prompts in the product; voice is optional
  • Needs AI evals, privacy review, and clear rules

Tradeoff: Could be huge if it works. Lots of research, privacy, and risk.

Several sprints. Treat as a stretch goal.

Must keep: SSO, role-based tasks, manager visibility, Chrome, WCAG AA, existing design tokens, no new mobile app.

Mix ideas: Hot tracks with Spicy practice on one workflow. Ship Mild now and Spicy later. Spicy UI with Inferno's ready-or-not score only.
